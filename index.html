How to use a textbook-level course

Read one lecture, reconstruct its derivations with the text closed, and solve its examples before checking the printed calculation. Use the worksheets to consolidate a method, the practice problems to select methods independently, and the quiz to test retention. The source estimates five weeks; this expanded treatment is best paced by understanding rather than a fixed time promise.

Equations are pre-rendered from LaTeX into embedded SVG. Diagrams are original SVG with text labels and captions. Browser search finds the prose and problem statements. Use the browser’s print command for a paper or PDF copy; expand any solutions you want included before printing, since print handling of collapsed details varies by browser.

Two numbering systems are retained: five course modules and 30 lectures. The source calls lectures 2–30 “Module 2” through “Module 30”; the coverage table preserves those exact titles while the textbook uses “Lecture” to avoid ambiguity.

Scope, notation, and limits

SI units are the default. Tension is positive for truss members. Each beam, cable, or contact model declares its own directions before using signed equations. Equilibrium does not by itself establish strength, stability, or contact feasibility; the course explicitly distinguishes these tests.

The course covers the listed statics subjects with supporting derivations, examples, and a cumulative assessment. Administrative entries explain the scope of this self-study edition.
